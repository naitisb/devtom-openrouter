Key findings
1

Format matters more than you'd think

MCQ and free-response tasks reveal different capability profiles. Claude Sonnet 4-6 scores 96% on free-response but collapses to 33% on MCQ — a format-specific failure invisible in aggregate scores. Across families, MCQ is generally easier but exposes more catastrophic failures.

2

Developmental order doesn't predict LLM difficulty

Items validated at older developmental ages are not harder for LLMs (rho = 0.049, p = 0.59). Emotion Recognition (age 3–4) is easiest, but Faux Pas Detection (age 9–11) is hardest while Irony (age 8–10) is among the easiest. LLMs acquire ToM-like abilities through a different pathway than children.

3

Scale helps, but isn't everything

Llama 70B outperforms 8B by ~10 percentage points. Qwen improves dramatically from 1.5 to 3 series (48% → 95%). But the top 8B models (Qwen3-8B at 95%) rival or exceed some 70B models. Architecture and training data matter as much as parameter count.

4

The ceiling is real — and it's low

The best models function at a ~8-year-old equivalent on free-response (IRT mapping). On MCQ, age-equivalents drop to ~6–7 years. The benchmark's hardest items (Faux Pas Detection, First-Order False Belief) still differentiate top models, but the gap between hardest item and best model indicates we need harder items.
